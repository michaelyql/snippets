# Questions

## Easy

Q: Two fair three-sided dice (faces 1, 2, 3) are rolled and their sum $S$ taken. Before the roll you choose a number $k$ from 1 to 6. If $k\leq S$ you are paid $k$; otherwise nothing. Which $k$ maximizes your expected payout, and what is that value? Enter the maximal expected payout as a reduced fraction.

A: $k=3$ and $k=4$ tie, each with expected value $\frac{8}{3}\approx2.67$. Choosing $k$ pays when $S\geq k$ and nothing otherwise, so its expected payout is $k\cdot P(S\geq k)$. The two dice give $3\cdot 3=9$ equally likely rolls. Counted by sum: 2 in 1 way, 3 in 2 ways, 4 in 3 ways, 5 in 2 ways, 6 in 1 way. Adding these counts from the top down gives the tail probabilities $P(S\geq 2)=1$, $P(S\geq 3)=\frac{8}{9}$, $P(S\geq 4)=\frac{6}{9}$, $P(S\geq 5)=\frac{3}{9}$, $P(S\geq 6)=\frac{1}{9}$. Multiplying by $k$, the six choices are worth $1\cdot 1=1$, $2\cdot 1=2$, $3\cdot \frac{8}{9}=\frac{8}{3}$, $4\cdot \frac{6}{9}=\frac{8}{3}$, $5\cdot \frac{3}{9}=\frac{5}{3}$, $6\cdot \frac{1}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$ ,  so the maximum $\frac{8}{3}$ is reached at both $k=3$ and $k=4$.

Q: A venue prints trades as a Poisson process at 6 per hour. You connect at a uniformly random instant. What is your expected wait until the next print?

A: 10 minutes. By memorylessness, the wait from any fixed instant to the next arrival is $Expo(\lambda)$ with $\lambda=6$ per hour, so the expected wait is $\frac{1}{\lambda}$ hours, which is 10 minutes.

Q: You have an unlimited supply of 20-sided dice numbered 1 to 20 and must choose a number $n$ of them to roll simultaneously, winning if the throw shows exactly one or exactly two 1s. What $n$ maximizes your probability of winning?

A: $n=28$.
